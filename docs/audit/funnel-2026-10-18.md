## A. The funnel

**How to read it.** Each loss row's % is of the count entering that stage (what is left after the rows above it); sub-rows are % of their parent. The order follows the brief, not the code: in the pipeline the text-repeat check runs first, then pass 1 (PASS1 route only), then the fast-track repeat check and D20 (before pass 2), then pass 2. Pending rows never reached any of those stages, so the last column also gives each loss as a share of all filing rows. `needs_review` is a flag, not a gate: what keeps a card out of the feed is `held` (the feed query is `WHERE c.held = 0`).

## D. Where correct cards are most likely lost (my reading)

_This section is interpretation. The numbers under each point are measured; the judgement that a card would have been correct is mine, from headlines and stored reasons, not from re-reading the PDFs._

Ranked by how many correct cards each stage probably loses. The evidence tables follow this list.

**1. Held cards whose only fault is a figure the pipeline gave the model (probably 40 to 60 cards; the biggest lever).**
118 of 451 cards (26%) are held. 76 of them have `needs_review = 0`: they pass every review rule in the blueprint,
and the only thing holding them is an unfound ₹ amount that the second provider did not clear. In the code
(`pipeline.py`), `held = review.status == "UNRESOLVED" and not only_style`. A second opinion is asked for whenever
`amounts_ungrounded` is non-empty, but `needs_review` does not count that list, so the hold is stricter than the review flag.
77 cards are held for "number not found" and nothing else (30 of them for one distinct figure). For **40 of the 77**, every
unfound figure sits next to one of the company-level denominators that `signals.scale_block` writes into the Pass 2
prompt (market cap, TTM revenue, net worth, net debt, order book), or is a quantified-impact input such as
`market_cap_cr=391.58` or `ttm_revenue=4343`. `ground()` checks figures against the filing, the headline, the subject and
the memory view, but not against the scale block or the company's price data. So the model uses the proportions we told it to
use, and the check calls them invented. Examples are b94e552a and 0f8362e3 (Shivam market cap 391.58), 58df06b2 (CSB
market cap 5,392), 0e2915bc (SML TTM revenue 2,504) and b5b9d96b (Shivam net debt 362.51).
The round-1 audit points the same way, though it judged older versions of these cards: 8 of the 12 audited "number-only"
holds were OK or MINOR, against 81% of published cards and 1 of 11 cards held for other reasons.
Not all of these are right. Some denominators are wrong in memory: Kanoria's net worth of ₹531.36 Cr comes from the 10x
slip noted in loop round 3c, and it holds c913f4c4 and 371cb9bc. So the fix is to ground scale-block figures against the
scale block, not to stop holding them: hold a card when its denominator differs from the scale block's, and release it
when the two match. Figures the model computes (sums, differences, shares) are a smaller group. The strict test, which
uses only figures the card states that are also in the filing, explains 14% of unfound figures against 6% for decoys.
Examples are SML 0fd4cf53's April–August volume of 2,052 and Kanoria 0758a110's 19.13.

**2. D20 lets the first results document decide the whole quarter (19 of the 43 quarters with D20 duplicates have no
published results card).**
- In 17 quarters the document that owns the quarter has a held card, so the quarter's other documents (statement, press
  release, presentation, integrated filing) are marked duplicates and nothing is published. The owner is usually the
  board outcome, and the dropped ones include the statement itself: SML c5e91c20, Kanoria 8722e04a, Shivam d82696b0 and

next document, or make the statement or XBRL document the owner; apply D20 only to a filing whose own text is a results
statement.
