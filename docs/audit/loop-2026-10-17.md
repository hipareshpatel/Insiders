# The 2026-10-17 loop: twenty filings, read five times

The owner's method (2026-10-17): work card quality in rounds of ten to twenty, audit the round, find
the root cause of each defect, fix it, then read the **same filings again**. A fresh sample would
measure the fix and a change of sample at once.

The twenty are round 1's WRONG cards, chosen by `scripts/rerun.py --from-audit docs/audit/round-1.json
--verdict WRONG --limit 20`: a stratified sample that covers every error class before it doubles up on
any, because the rarest classes sit on single cards. They span 2025-01-23 to 2026-08-11, eight
companies, nine event types and all ten classes. `process --news-ids` reads only them. The model
weights were left alone between rounds, or the comparison would measure the models.

## What each round changed

| Round | Fixes added |
|---|---|
| 2a | none - the starting point, on the code as it stood after reading order and the quote contract |
| 2b | the XBRL override no longer writes the quarter's figure under the year's label; a change of more than a thousand percent is read whole; the deterministic fixers run on a card adopted from the second provider, and the record beside a card describes the card that was published |
| 2c | a bare label follows the period the card is about; a figure replaced from another period loses the change that belonged to the one it replaced; a quoted row is read at a unit |
| 2d | the unit comes from the filing rather than the XBRL table put in front of it; a day of the month is not a figure; how a card quoted the filing no longer holds it back by itself |
| 2e | one rule for both quote checks, rather than two that had drifted apart within a day |
| 2f | a notable change answers for the period it reports, not the one it is measured against |
| 3a | pages of figures read by local OCR first, checked cell by cell against the text layer (D29; ran on 881e6a1, before a page covered by an image counted as a scan) |
| 3b | a scan's own OCR layer under its image is not the PDF's text; a results XBRL file tagged in the wrong unit is put right (Jubilant re-seeded); raw answers stored |
| 3c | a change computed from its own line, figures code computed, rounding to the card's last digit, the YoY of the period the card names, two-column pages read block by block for quotes, near-verbatim quotes as style (e4ea180, 34b3942) |

## What it cost and what it bought

Nothing here is a verdict on whether a card is right - that is audit round 2, still to run. These are
the counts the code itself produces: how many cards were written, how many were held back from the
feed, and what each hold was for. A held card is not lost: it goes to the review queue.

| Round | Cards | Held | The commonest hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2a | 14 | 5 | a figure not found in the filing or memory (5) |
| 2b | 13 | 10 | a figure its own quote does not carry (29) |
| 2c | 16 | 11 | the same (28) |
| 2d | 13 | 11 | the same (25) |
| 2e | 18 | 17 | the same (39) |
| 2f | 12 | 6 | the same (19) |
| 3a | 16 | 10 | the same (8) |
| 3b | 15 | 7 (6 with the YoY check fixed after it) | the same (7) |
| 3c | 15 | 5 | the same (5) |

Holding went up, not down, and the reason is worth stating plainly rather than explaining away.

Two thirds of it is the checks becoming honest. Before 2b, three of our own fixers were quietly
rewriting cards - a full year's revenue replaced by the quarter's, a change spliced into the middle of
another - and the record stored beside a card described the card that had been discarded. Cards that
should have been held were passing, and the audit trail said the opposite of what shipped.

The rest is the quote contract, added the day before, being stricter than it is accurate. It began by
reading period labels and days of the month as figures, comparing a card's crore against a statement's
lakh digit by digit, and taking its unit from the XBRL table rather than the document. Each of those is
now fixed; 2e then went backwards because the two quote checks were made one and the surviving rule was
the stricter of the two, which asked a notable change's quote to carry the figure from the period it is
measured against. That one is fixed too.

What is left, and what the next loop is for: 23 of 2e's 39 are key numbers, why-it-matters and
what-happened whose figures are right and whose quote does not carry them - an analytical sentence
drawing on more than the one row it quotes. The contract needs calibrating against the 40/8/3 baseline
rather than loosening by feel.

## Round 3a: OCR as the first reader (2026-10-17, late)

The same twenty filings, read with D29: on the sixteen carded filings 37 figure pages went to RapidOCR;
34 kept OCR's rows (checked against the text layer) and 3 - an investor presentation's charts, where OCR
misses axis ticks and small data labels - kept the text layer. Card count moves between rounds with pass 1 and the duplicate checks
(12 to 18 across 2a-3a), so holds are compared per card and by reason.

Did reading figure pages with OCR change what a card could stand on? Every figure each card states, and
every figure its holds name, was looked for in the text the analysis read and in the plain text layer
(scratch `ocr_attribution.py`): **no card states a figure the text layer had and OCR's reading lost.**
Two cards gained: CSB Bank's total income row reads "3,51, 183" in the text layer and "3,51,183" from OCR,
so the model's quote of the row is now exact; Kanoria's continuing-operations rows are on a scan whose own
text layer reads "(a,305)", and OCR read them clean. No hold traced so far comes from the reading:

**Found on the way, worse than any hold:** Jubilant Pharmova's cards say revenue +979% and PAT +11,403%
YoY (2f had +996.8%). The company's own Q4FY24 XBRL tags its figures at a tenth of their value - revenue
1,758,600,000 INR with the rounding declared "Lakhs", for a statement in millions - and the memory seed
takes XBRL as exact, so FY24 and Q4FY24 sit in memory at ₹670.29 Cr and ₹175.86 Cr against ₹6,702.9 Cr

amounts sit an exact power of ten from the company's other quarters is put right (or refused) before it
reaches memory, then the affected filings are read again.

## Round 3b (2026-10-17, 23:05)

The same twenty, with a scan's under-image OCR layer no longer taken for the PDF's text and Jubilant's
mis-scaled Q4FY24 file put right (in memory by re-seeding, and live for the card's comparatives - the run
logged it). 15 cards, 7 held. Jubilant's FY25 card now says revenue +7.9% YoY (₹7,234.5 Cr against
₹6,702.9 Cr) where it said +979%; its PAT +1,050% and EPS +988% are real (FY24 PAT ₹72.7 Cr, and FY25
carries a large exceptional gain). No raw-copy warnings: answers are stored again (F55).

One of its holds was new and false: "+7.9% YoY is a quarter-on-quarter change". The YoY/QoQ check compared
a card's "% YoY" with the latest quarter's changes only, so a full-year or half-year change could never
match a YoY, and a loss narrowing (Shivam's FY25 PAT, -48.04 against -50.2, "+4.3% YoY (less negative)")
has no XBRL change at all. Fixed: a YoY may match any period's YoY in the file, or a change computed on a
loss. Applied offline to the stored cards (scratch `period_mixups` re-check, the cards' own XBRL with
comparatives rebuilt as the pipeline does): every one of the YoY/QoQ holds of rounds 3a (5) and 3b (5)
was such a change and clears; each flagged change was checked as a real YoY (Shivam's revenue -3.3%,
469.47 to 453.98; Novartis's H1 PAT +12.3%). Shivam's FY25 card had no other hold, so 3b stands at 6 of 15.

Still open from these rounds, in order of cards held: figures the model computes (a net-debt change, a sum
of two considerations) quoted against the single row they came from; two-column letters read across both
columns; quotes that leave out one cell of the row they copy.

Released cards checked: Shivam's two (corrected by the second opinion) read right against their filings. Kanoria's

in unchecked and now grounds the new card.

Four defects, found by this round and going to the discard-and-hold audit (docs/audit/discard-paths.md):
1. the sibling-row rule matches digits without the card's unit, so Jubilant's ₹3,595 Cr passed as "from
   another row of the same table" against a statement's 3,595 million;
2. the unit-slip fixer skips a fix when the corrected reading is within 0.5% of any figure in the filing - on a
   long statement that happens by chance (Jubilant: 35,950 and 7,960 both had a neighbour);
3. a balance-sheet figure from a card enters memory with no scale check (Kanoria's net worth);
4. a fact's year-ago figure from a far row of the same statement holds a right card (CSB).
